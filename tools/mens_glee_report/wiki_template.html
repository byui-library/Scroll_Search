Men’s Glee (also called the Male Glee, Male Glee Club, Men’s Glee and Boys’ Glee) was the men’s vocal ensemble of Ricks Academy and Ricks College. It appears in the student newspapers from 1916 to 1958. Its work was carried on by the later Men’s Chorus and Men’s Choir (1964–1988).

Overview

The student newspapers first mention a Male Glee in 1916, when the music department already had several “glees.” Through the 1920s and 1930s the Male Glee sang at devotionals, commencement and Leadership Week, performed for the Rotary Club and the Chamber of Commerce, and entered the annual stunt fests. From the late 1930s the men’s and ladies’ glees often combined, most visibly for Handel’s “Messiah” each December and for General Conference in Salt Lake City in 1940 and 1948. The name “Male Glee Club” appears for the last time in 1958 under Dr. Chester Hill. After that the men sang as a Men’s or Male Chorus, organized in 1970 under Dr. Richard Robison and later led by Clyde Luke. In 1980 and 1983 Ricks sent a men’s choir of about 400 voices to the priesthood session of General Conference.

History

1916–1929: Ricks Academy and Ricks Normal College

The music department ran separate men’s and ladies’ glees. The Male Glee sang at devotionals, for the stake priesthood school and for the Rotary Club. In April 1928 it gave a 30-minute concert after the Chamber of Commerce banquet at the Idamont Hotel, and it closed the 1928 commencement with “Blow Ye Winds.” In May 1928 the combined glees under Prof. F. F. Beale replaced the annual opera with Coleridge-Taylor’s cantata “Hiawatha’s Wedding Feast.”

1934–1940: The Purple Flash and early Viking Scroll years

The glee clubs sang at stake conferences, P.T.A. meetings and assemblies. The Male Glee Club took second place in the 1935 stunt fest with “A Pirate Ship,” and the Men’s Glee performed “If Men Entertain at Football Teas Like Women Do” in 1936. Under Prof. John M. Anderson the two glees, about 100 voices, presented the “Messiah” from 1937. In April 1940, 170 singers from the Glee Club sang at General Conference.

1946–1958: After the war

In 1946 the Boys’ Glee met twice a week and joined the Girls’ Glee as a combined chorus on Fridays. In 1947 the Male Glee sang “Dear Land of Hope,” “House by the Side of the Road” and “Grey Bays” during Leadership Week, and a men’s chorus sang on Anderson’s 1949 radio broadcasts. In 1958 Dr. Chester Hill’s Male Glee Club sang Richter’s “The Creation” and represented the “Unconquerable Viking” in the Homecoming assembly.

1964–1976: Men’s Chorus and Male Chorus

An Associated Men’s Chorus led by Murell Johnson sang at a 1964 assembly. A Men’s Chorus was organized under Dr. Richard W. Robison in 1970 and gave joint Christmas, fall and spring concerts with the Women’s Choir. By 1972 Clyde Luke directed the Men’s Choir. It met three mornings a week, sang at Baccalaureate and in the “Messiah,” and appeared in televised Christmas devotionals.

1980–1988: General Conference men’s choirs

Ricks organized a men’s choir of students, faculty and staff, about 400 voices, for the priesthood session of General Conference in October 1980 and again in April 1983 (more than 390 voices), both directed by Dr. Richard Robison. In December 1988 Ricks was invited to provide a men’s choir for the April 1989 conference.

Directors

  • Prof. Harry Dean: Male Glee, 1927–29
  • Prof. Billeter: Ladies’ Glee and Leadership Week music, 1927–28
  • Prof. F. F. Beale: combined glee clubs, “Hiawatha’s Wedding Feast,” 1928
  • Prof. John M. Anderson: glee clubs, “Messiah,” men’s chorus radio broadcasts, 1937–1950
  • Dr. Chester W. Hill: Male Glee Club, 1958
  • Murell Johnson: Associated Men’s Chorus, 1964
  • Dr. Richard W. Robison: Men’s Chorus, 1970–71; General Conference men’s choirs, 1980 and 1983
  • Clyde E. Luke: Men’s Choir / Male Chorus, 1971–73

Repertoire

  • “Blow Ye Winds” (1928 commencement)
  • “Hiawatha’s Wedding Feast,” Coleridge-Taylor (combined glees, 1928)
  • Handel, “Messiah” (combined glees, annually from 1937)
  • “Dear Land of Hope,” “House by the Side of the Road,” “Grey Bays” (1947)
  • “The Creation,” Richter, with a reading by Joseph Catmull (1958)
  • “Almighty God of Our Fathers” (1971)
  • “Adam and Eve” and “Dry Bones” (male chorus numbers, 1976)
